# What our research assumes, and what happens if it is wrong

Assumption audit, board task #27 (M6), H12, 2026-11-17. Sensitivity runs: `experiments/assumption_audit/` (pre-registered, `docs/prereg/assumption_audit.md`).

## The answer first

**The structural findings rest on few assumptions, and we have now stress-tested most of them. The plan to read sound values rests on four assumptions that are each likely to be wrong.**

**Structural findings (README #1, #3, #5, #6, #10a, #11).** These are statements about how signs are ordered and used. They survive:
- 12 alternative sign lists (`signlist_robustness`);
- 10% of signs misread at random or as look-alikes;
- half the objects read in the wrong direction;
- stricter near-copy removal;
- dropping every object with a doubtful sign.

They do not depend on the signs being language. If the signs are not language, the facts stay and only the words change: "grammar" becomes "fixed ordering", "word sign" becomes "symbol".

**What does depend on assumptions:**

1. **Reading direction.** This is known well for the corpus as a whole. But #7 (L-R reversals), #10b (fixed pair order) and the slot names opener/closer used in #3 and #9 need it for nearly every text. 10% of objects read backwards already pushes #7 and #10b below their bars.
2. **The words we use for the findings.** "Grammatical" (#1, #3), "logo-syllabic" (#2), "name cores" (#10) and "title" (#9) assume two things: the signs write language, and seal texts are mostly personal names. Our own tests cannot show the first (language vs code is undecidable at this size) and argue against the second.

**Load-bearing for the whole programme**, the route to sound values in the root goal R and M1-M3:

| assumption | chance it is wrong | if wrong |
|---|---|---|
| A1 the signs write a language | medium | no sound values exist to find; R, M1, M2, M3 end |
| A8 Gulf-type round seals write foreign names | **high** | M1 as the main line ends (#22-#25 already moved the effect to a round-seal genre) |
| A7 seal texts are mostly owners' names | **medium-high** | crib attacks have no target; the onomasticon lists do not apply |
| A9 the language has a known relative with a usable lexicon | **high** | every lexicon check, including M2 benches and any Dravidian or Indo-Aryan key, has nothing to check against |
| A13 our bench can recognise true sound values | **high (our own results say so)** | the goal's checkable form "beats chance on our bench" cannot be met even by a correct key |

**The three assumptions most likely to be wrong:**
1. **A8, Gulf-type round seals carry foreign names.** If wrong, it takes down M1's premise and the "another language" reading of #4.
2. **A13, the bench can certify sound values.** If wrong, the goal as worded is unreachable.
3. **A9, the language has a usable known relative.** If wrong, every lexicon-based check is empty.

A7 (names) is a close fourth.

## Which ranked findings survive if an assumption fails

Key:
- **S**: survives as stated.
- **W**: survives, but the wording must change.
- **s**: sensitive (misses its bar under some variants, effect kept).
- **F**: falls or loses its meaning.
- **-**: not affected.

Columns: A1 the signs are not language; A2 the sign list is wrong; A3 transcription has random errors up to 10%; A4 direction is wrong for many texts; A5 several languages or systems are pooled; A7 seal texts are not names; A8 round seals are not foreign; A10 the texts are not independent; A12 the order is a format, not a grammar.

## Suggested rewording of README (not edited by this audit)

- **#1:** "Sign order is fixed and structured, not positional: real order beats every permutation of its own signs far above chance (...). This holds with half the texts read backwards (2.6x chance) and with 10% of signs misread (5x). It shows sequential structure, which fits a grammar or a fixed formula or format equally."
- **#2:** "If the signs write language, the inventory fits a logo-syllabic or mostly logographic script. About 400 signs under Mahadevan's list (712 labels in ICIT)..." Our evidence (no phonetic spelling detected) leans to the logographic end, so "logo-syllabic" alone overstates what we know.
- **#3:** Replace "with a grammatical effect" with "with a positional effect". Add: "the effect size is the same under every sign list, but the p-value misses 0.05 when the fish are lumped."
- **#4:** Change "Fits Indus signs used for another language (Parpola), but pseudo-writing is not excluded" to "Fits three readings that our data cannot separate: another language (Parpola), a round-seal genre in the Indus language, or pseudo-writing."
- **#7:** Add: "This depends on the direction labels being right for nearly every line: with 10% of objects flipped at random it falls to 0.69-0.72."
- **#9:** Add: "the role names (title, unit, noun) are labels for positional classes, not tested meanings."
- **#10:** Change "Name cores" to "Text cores (the non-frame part; not shown to be names)" throughout. To the fixed-order sentence add: "agreement falls from 0.79 to 0.69-0.74 if 10% of texts are read in the wrong direction, so it assumes direction is known text by text."
- **#11:** "lexical class" assumes language; "a class of signs that substitute for each other" is the tested claim.
- **Root goal R** (board/README.md, not README): replace "beat chance on our bench" with "supported by an anchor outside the texts (a name tied to an object, or a bilingual) and correctly predicting texts not used to fit them". Our own benches show a text-internal score cannot certify a true key at Indus size.

## Checks recommended next, not run

In order of value for cost:
1. **Order test on fixed-format corpora (A12).** Run #1's permutation test on the short_labels corpora. This is in hand and takes seconds per corpus; it needs a prereg. If formats also reach ~40%, reword #1 for good.
2. **M77 vs ICIT token disagreement rate (A3).** Measure it on bridge-aligned texts. In hand; it gives a real transcription error rate to set against the 10% tolerance found here.
3. **#3 on complete single-line texts only (A16).** In hand, ~15 s; needs a prereg.
4. **Board ledger rows merged and BH re-run (A11b).** This is the lead's job.
5. **T1-T9 per object type and per period (A5).** In hand.
6. **Photo-based direction coding of left-to-right and disputed lines (A4).** Needs CISI crops and human or blind raters.
7. **Allograph clustering from photographs (A2).** Needs ≥30 px/mm images (museum masters), which we cannot get yet.
