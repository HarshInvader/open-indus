# What would move the Indus question: the gates we hit, and the material that would reopen each

2026-11-26. A two-page summary for outside experts. Every route below was run on open data until it reached a gate or a kill test (10b.20 was closed at review, before its prereg was sealed). Each row gives the number the test needed, the number open data gave, and the material that would reopen it. Details and full records are in `docs/NEGATIVE_ATLAS.md` (section numbers in the first column) and in each experiment's `RESULTS.md`.

## Governing point

No route on open data can now move the odds of reading the script. Most stop for lack of primary material of one specific kind; two (word division from sign gaps, and judging keys by dictionary fit) stop because no valid measure exists. So the most useful help from experts is access to particular objects, photographs, labelled corpora or expert measurements, listed in the last column.

## Routes toward reading the script

| Route (record) | What the test needed | What open data gave | What would reopen it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anchor pairs: an Indus legend tied to a known name (`anchor_crossread`, `experiments/anchor_sources_scope/SCOPE.md`) | More than 15 anchor pairs. On clean Ur III legends the cross-read passes at 15 pairs (20 of 20 true sets) with reading direction known; with 20% of signs unread it passes only 10 of 20 at 15 pairs | 0 anchor-grade pairs in the sources opened; the ceiling is single digits even if every request is granted. YBC 5447 is not an anchor as published (Hallo & Buchanan 1965: a later Dilmun type) | An unpublished archive of Gulf or Mesopotamian tablets with stamp impressions never yet photographed and a named sealer. First: edge and impression photos of P453801 (Nisaba 15, 0371) and P516366 (CUSAS 40, 1582) |
| Same rule on a read script, Indo-Greek coins (`crossread_coins`) | 30 names with both legends | 28 names (26 BIGR, 2 Wikipedia) | Licensed access to gandhari.org's 337 coin legends, or another open catalogue, under a new prereg |
| Certifying a key by restoring held-out signs with a candidate-language lexicon (`lexicon_ceiling`; atlas 1.1) | A lexicon covering about 90% of held-out words (the true Old Assyrian key gained in 18 of 20 draws at 91% coverage) | A related, distant lexicon covers 32%, and with it the true key showed nothing. For Indus no lexicon of known match can exist | None stated: no lexicon of known match can exist for Indus |
| Published phonetic keys judged by dictionary fit (atlas 1.1) | A test that a correct key passes reliably | On dictionary fit, the true Linear B key at Indus length and 74% coverage does not beat its shuffles (2.26 against a 99th percentile of 2.30); on the stricter IC test, a correct planted Sanskrit key passes only 10 to 25% of the time on the real corpus | A key with one value per sign, fixed before scoring, that beats keys fitted to noise with the same freedom; forward predictions on new finds; covariation with objects and pictures |
| Word division from sign gaps (10b.23) | A blind measurer of sign gaps at ICC ≥ 0.70 against expert boxes, recall ≥ 0.85 | Blind agents reached ICC 0.24 to 0.61 and recall 0.13 to 0.79 | Expert sign boxes drawn blind to the readings, for a read script and for Indus, or a measurer validated at ICC ≥ 0.70 |
| Word division from line wraps (10b.10, 10b.27) | 200 confirmed splits (power 0.87) | 25 confirmed (22 wraps found in both editions, 3 face splits); the capacity null can never reach p < 0.01 | At least 200 splits confirmed from photographs, plus a capacity null with a spread |
| Validating a method on read scripts first (10b.20, 10b.21, 10b.22) | Tournament: enough independent families to hold out cells with useful power. Sign-type classifier: 3 labelled families. Sealing model: 8 corpora in 4 families | 3 families, so at most 5 held-out cells and about 27% power at a +0.10 advantage (tournament); 2 labelled families (classifier); 4 sealing corpora in 3 families | A fourth open family or a blind bench front end (tournament); a third labelled family, e.g. a larger clean Linear B release (classifier); open Hittite (Nişantepe) or Sasanian bulla corpora, or a licensed Chinese seal corpus (sealing model) |
| Precedent: has any script with Indus's profile been read? (`decipher_reference_class`) | One read script with no known language, no outside anchor, no borrowed values and no word division | 0 of 26 read and 0 of 14 unread scripts share that profile (Old Turkic and Safaitic are possible matches, with some features not stated) | (A fact about history, not a data gap) |

## The specific asks this table supports

- Edge and impression photographs of P453801 and P516366 (P453801 asked of Owen, Rositani and CDLI; the P516366 request to the Iraq heritage board bounced).
- Access to an unpublished archive of Gulf or Mesopotamian tablets sealed with stamps and naming the sealer.
- CISI vol. 3 (Parpola) photographs: West Asian impressions and Harappa sealings.
- Expert-drawn sign boxes, blind to the readings, on a sample of Indus and read-script objects.

Anyone with one of these can reopen a closed route. We would re-register the test before looking and publish the outcome either way.
