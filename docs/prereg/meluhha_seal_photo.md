# What we will test if a photo of a Meluhha-household seal impression arrives (sealed 2026-11-23, before any photo)

Agent `paper`. Written so that a match cannot be fitted after the fact. Suggested by an independent Fable read on 2026-11-23, after the neighbouring-scripts scan found no route on open data (see RESEARCH_STATE, "Neighbouring-fields scan").

## Objects
- P453801 (Nisaba 15, 0371) and P516366 (CUSAS 40, 1582): oil rations for the household of Nanasa and Samar, "men of Meluhha". The seal impressions are recorded as illegible. Photos were requested on 2026-11-23 from A. Rositani and CDLI.
- Any Gulf- or Indus-type impression that Yale's curator reports on a sealed Ur or Larsa tablet (asked on 2026-11-23).

## Step 1. What kind of seal (decided by others, not us)
The seal type is taken from a named specialist's reading (cylinder, Gulf round stamp, Indus square stamp, other). We do not classify it ourselves; our own image tests failed on YBC 5447 (atlas 9.2). A Mesopotamian cylinder ends the test: there is nothing to read.

## Step 2. Transcription (blind)
If Indus signs are legible, two readers transcribe them independently into Mahadevan 1977 sign numbers. Each reader works from the photo alone, without seeing the tablet's text or this file's predictions, and gives a confidence for each sign. Only signs that both readers agree on are used.

## Step 3. Name test (only if the seal could be Nanasa's or Samar's)
The tablet names a household, and the seal may belong to an official, not to a Meluhhan. So the test applies only if the specialist or the tablet's text ties the seal to Nanasa or Samar.
- **Prediction for Nanasa (na-na-sa).** If the Indus script spells this name phonetically, the seal text contains a doubled sign followed by a different sign (pattern AAB) within one line.
- **Prediction for Samar (sa-ma-ar).** There is no structural prediction: the pattern ABC fits almost any text. A Samar seal is recorded but not scored.
- **Chance baseline, computed now from the corpus.** Of 2,593 seal and sealing texts in M77, 98 contain AAB within a line (3.8%), and 146 contain any doubled sign (5.6%). Median length is 4 signs.
- **Reading the result.**
  - AAB on a seal tied to Nanasa: a lead at about p = 0.04 for one object. It is not a reading. It goes to red team and to experts.
  - No AAB: a weak negative for a fully phonetic spelling of the name. It is not a negative for the script.
- **Not allowed after the photo:** changing the name forms, the pattern, or the sign list, or treating any other sign pattern as a match.

## Step 4. Gulf seals without Indus signs
Record the find as a digraphic lead in atlas 9.2, with the iconography as the specialist describes it. No reading is attempted.
