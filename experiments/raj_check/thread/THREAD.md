1/ As much as I'd love this to be true: the Indus script has not been cracked.

We reran this paper's own code on its own data.

Put the signs' pictures on random wrong signs → Tamil scores the same.
Feed it Japanese surnames → 60% of Tamil's score.

🧵 [image 1]
---
2/ How it "reads" a sign:
An AI describes each sign ("jar"). An AI translator gives Tamil words. Then every Tamil name part that merely sounds similar is added, and a solver picks whatever makes the seals look like Tamil names.

59% of final readings are sound-alikes. [image 3]
---
3/ Test 1: we moved each sign's picture words onto a random wrong sign. 50 times.

Tamil's score: 223 → 224. (p = 0.51)

If the test doesn't care what a sign depicts, it can't show the signs depict Tamil words.
---
4/ Test 2: a language with zero Indus link, whose names are also built from drawable things (Yamada = mountain + rice field).

Japanese surnames: +134, 60% of Tamil, beating it in 4 of 10 runs.
Sanskrit, which the paper "rules out": −13.

It measures naming style, not language.
---
5/ (Honest caveat: by the bar we fixed before running, Japanese just misses, 7 of 10 runs above chance where we required 8. Tamil's real edge is consistency. Still nowhere near "cracked".)
---
6/ The "proof": signs read "copper" and "armlet" show up on copper tablets and bangles.

But copper tablets came in sets with the same text. The 21 "copper" tablets carry just 3 distinct inscriptions. Count each once: p = 0.19. [image 2]
