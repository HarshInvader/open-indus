1/ As much as I'd love this to be true: the Indus script has not been "cracked" 🧵

We downloaded the paper's code and ran it.

Then we swapped Tamil for Japanese, a language with no known link to the Indus.

The code "decoded" the seals in Japanese too. [image A_one_seal.png]
---
2/ The paper's own score (how much better real names fit the seals than scrambled ones):

Tamil: +223
Japanese: +134
Sanskrit: −13 (the paper rules it out)

Japanese beats Tamil in 4 of 10 test runs. Same code, same seals. [image B_scores.png]
---
3/ How it "reads" a sign: the sign shows a pot. Tamil for pot is kuṭam. The method swaps it for kuṭi, a word that only sounds similar, and reads "clan village".

59% of the final readings work like this. That freedom is why Japanese fits too. [image D_method.png]
---
4/ Test: we moved each sign's picture words onto a random wrong sign. 50 times.

Tamil's score: 223 → 224. (p = 0.51)

The test doesn't care what a sign depicts. So it can't show the signs depict Tamil words.
---
5/ Why Tamil "beats" Sanskrit: Sanskrit names are mostly single words, so there's little to fit (−13).

Give the method a language whose names are built from drawable things, like Japanese (Yamada = mountain + rice field), and it fits too.

It measures naming style, not language.
---
6/ The "proof": signs read "copper" and "armlet" show up on copper tablets and bangles.

But copper tablets came in sets with the same text. The 21 "copper" tablets carry just 3 distinct inscriptions. Count each once: p = 0.19. [image C_copies.png]
---
7/ To be fair: the author published everything (code, data, preregistered tests). Rare, and why we could check it in a day. It reproduces exactly. By the bar we fixed in advance, Japanese just misses (7 of 10 runs above chance, needed 8).

But fitting a key isn't decipherment.
---
8/ Bottom line: it beats random noise, but not the placebo that matters. Put the pictures on the wrong signs and it scores the same.

Real science needs a test that can fail, fixed in advance. That's how we work at Open Indus.
