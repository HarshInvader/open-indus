# Susa "text O" (Louvre Sb 9382, LEI 15/O): is it a bridge to the Indus script?

Read 2026-11-18. Sources (copies in `raw/`, gitignored, not redistributed):
- Desset's 2026 monograph on Linear Elamite (orientlab.net download 588; 182 pp.), printed pp. 44-47 (§2.2.4 LEI 15/O, Table 2.1) and Table 1.1 (catalogue of 45 LE inscriptions known in 2026).
- Hatamti Linear Elamite database: document list and doc 15 (drawing of both sides, photograph of side A).
- Louvre Collections page for Sb 9382 (photo at 1126 x 1500). The museum confirms no photographs of the tablet's edges exist.

## What the book says
- Eight lines, seven on side A and one on side B; probably written left to right. No sign repeats, so it is most likely a **sign list**, not a running text.
- Earlier views: a separate, older script (Salvini 1998); excluded from LE (Desset 2012); a stage between Proto-Elamite and Linear Elamite (Hinz 1969, Meriggi 1971); a religious text outside true writing (Dahl 2009).
- **Fuls (2024, Iranian Journal of Archaeological Studies 14: 3-16)** proposed Indus signs in it. Desset: several of those matches are "rather forced", and most of the supposed Indus-like signs carry the **dots at stroke ends that mark Linear Elamite writing and never occur in the Indus script**.
- Table 2.1 matches 21 of its signs to Proto-Elamite (Dahl numbers) and/or Linear Elamite signs; none to Indus.
- Date: excavation context points to the late 3rd millennium BCE; letter forms point to the beginning of the millennium.

## What we see in the photo and drawing
Dots at line ends appear on most signs in every line, as Desset says. Shapes such as the crossed box, the jar-like sign and the "fish"-like curve in line 7 have loose look-alikes in the Indus sign list, but simple geometric shapes recur in most early scripts, and a sign list with no repeats gives no context to test a match.

## Catalogue check
Of the 45 LE inscriptions (Table 1.1; Hatamti list), none is recorded with Indus signs or an Indus-style seal impression. The two seals in the LE corpus (LEI 22, a Gulf-type seal; LEI 45, unpublished) carry LE text only.

## Verdict for our project
The digraphic (two-script) lead through text O is **downgraded to "no support"**. Paying for new photographs is not justified: the Louvre has no edge views, and the face photographs are already public. Bilingual search (NEGATIVE_ATLAS 9.3) stays untestable.

## Museum photographs checked (2026-11-18)
The Louvre sent its full-resolution study photographs (5 TIFFs: 3 black-and-white raking-light shots, 4000 x 5500; 2 colour, 4888 x 6510; front and back). They are for study only and are not published here (publication needs RMN clearance); they sit in the gitignored `raw/louvre/`.
- Front, at full resolution: the drilled dots at stroke ends and corners are plain on nearly every sign, including the boxed, crossed and ladder-like signs most often compared with Indus signs. That matches Desset's point that these are Linear Elamite-style dots, which the Indus script does not use.
- Back: ruled into eight lines but almost empty: one line of four or five signs at one end, a few faint scratches and a deliberate round hole in the middle. Nothing on it looks like an Indus sequence.
- No new signs beyond the published drawing; no seal impression; edges were not photographed (none exist).
Verdict unchanged: lead closed.
