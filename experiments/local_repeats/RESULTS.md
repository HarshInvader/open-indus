# local_repeats: what are the repeated 4+ sign seal texts?

Pre-registered: `docs/prereg/local_repeats.md` (hashed in `docs/prereg/HASHES.txt` before any tabulation). Ledger rows `lr_t1`..`lr_t5` (confirmatory family of 5, Holm) and `lr_e1`, `lr_e2` (exploratory). Other experiment folders were only imported, never edited.

## Findings first

1. **The premise was half wrong: these repeats are not local.** There are 15 repeated texts of 4+ signs on 36 seals, which form 28 same-text pairs. 12 pairs (43%) are at one site; a random pair of 4+ sign seals is at one site 45% of the time. Per possible pair, a matching text is as likely between cities as within one (ratio 1.10).
   - The red team's "local like Ur III" figure (R_LOO 0.18) came from small reference sites. One Chanhu-daro pair and one Dholavira pair were scaled up against Mohenjo-daro's 683 seals. With Mohenjo-daro as the reference, R = 1.35.
   - Ur III repeated legends behave very differently: 95-98% of pairs are in the same archive, against 28% for random pairs.
2. **About 40% of the excess is one travelling formula.** The formula is opener M267/M391 + M99 + 3, 4 or 6 short strokes + M161 (in Wells numbering, W817/W861/W820 W002 W003/W004/W016 W390).
   - It accounts for 14 of the 34 objects. The bigram null expects 0.15.
   - It occurs at Mohenjo-daro, Harappa, Chanhu-daro, Nausharo and Dholavira, and also on a Kalibangan sealing and a Harappa moulded tablet.
   - The only part that varies is the numeral. That looks like a standard title or quantity formula, not a personal name.
   - The other 20 objects still exceed chance (20 vs 7.5 expected [2-16], p=0.002).
3. **None of the four explanations is clearly supported.**
   - (a) catalogue duplication: not supported. 0 of 12 same-site pairs are one object recorded twice.
   - (b) re-cut or replacement seals: partly. Most same-site pairs differ in size (7 of 12), but same-text pairs are *closer* in size than random pairs.
   - (c) office held by successive persons: untestable. Only 1 pair has a date. It does have one signature in its favour: repeated texts often have a one-sign-longer version at the same site.
   - (d) workshop templates: partly. Same-text pairs match on size and emblem, but only 2 of 12 pairs look like template copies.
4. **Repeated texts grow; they never shrink.**
   - 6 of 15 repeated texts have a one-sign-longer text at the same site, against 8 of 1,017 singleton texts (Fisher p=9e-9, Holm 3e-8).
   - None has a one-sign-shorter text at the same site, against 37 of 1,017 singletons.
   - The added sign is not always the same and lands in different positions: W400=M176 twice at the end, W033=M89 at the start, and W233, W142, W621.
   - Ur III repeated legends show this only weakly: 4.9% vs 2.4% overall, and 0 of 55 at 7 signs or fewer.

## Exploratory (not pre-registered)
- **E1, locality** (`explore.json`):
  - Same-site share of same-text pairs is 0.43 vs 0.45 for random pairs.
  - 53% of families sit at one site, vs 39% if the same objects were placed at random sites (p=0.18).
  - Re-running red-team R_LOO with each reference site alone: Mohenjo-daro 1.35, Harappa 0.28, Dholavira 0.07, Chanhu-daro 0.05. Dropping the two small references gives 0.49.
- **E2, formula decomposition:** with red-team N2 bigram null, 500 simulations: formula 14 vs 0.15; other 20 vs 7.5 [2-16]. Both p=0.002.
- **E3, merged-corpus 'time' as period:** 4 dated same-site pairs.
  - Same period: H-1032/H-62 (3C-2) and H-352/H-354 (3B/C).
  - Adjacent periods: H-1679/H-451 (3C vs 3C-2) and M-1790/M-854 (3B/C vs 3C-1).
  - Nothing is spread over centuries, but n=4.
- **E4, genre control:** with only square seals in the null, emblem identity still holds (0.60 vs 0.16, p=0.002). The size test drops to 7 pairs, so it is untestable. T1 may just say that a text type goes with a seal genre (square unicorn seals of 20-30 mm).
- **Ur III reference** (`ur3_ref.json`; 4,757 physical seals, 365 repeated legends):
  - 98% of repeated-legend pairs are in the same archive (95% at 7 signs or fewer); random pairs are in the same archive 28% of the time.
  - Of same-archive pairs, 53-56% fall in different reigns, against 66% for random pairs. Ur III repeats are mostly one person's (or namesakes') seals over years: type (b) with some (c).
  - Indus repeats look nothing like this.

## Caveats
- The sample is small: 12 same-site pairs and 9 with sizes. Every verdict is fragile.
- The photo check was not blind.
- Dimensions for Mohenjo-daro and Harappa come from 1930s reports, and many are rounded to 1/10 inch.
- The ICIT period field is empty for most objects, so the (c) test had no data.
- T5 may partly reflect commonness: repeated texts are built from common bigrams. But the shorter-text control is 0 of 15, which argues against commonness alone.
- The longer-variant search requires exact one-sign insertion.

## What this means
- The only repeat excess in seal texts is not a set of local personal seals.
- Its biggest part is one formula, the same everywhere, with a varying numeral. The rest are rare texts that tend to have a longer variant at the same site.
- That fits a stock of standard titles or formulas, written with optional extra signs, better than it fits individual owners.
- The red-team reading "re-cut seals of one holder, Ur III-like" should be withdrawn.
