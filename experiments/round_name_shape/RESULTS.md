# Round seal texts as phonetically spelled names: untestable at n (prereg `round_name_shape`, hashed 2026-11-23T18:08:06Z; code 18:08:42Z)

**Verdict: STOP at the power gate. Planted power is 0.22 against a bar of 0.8, so the real round set was not scored.**

## Why the test has no power
- Removing person and numeral signs leaves 28 non-empty residues out of the 35 round texts. They are short:
  - length 1: 6
  - length 2: 9
  - length 3: 7
  - length 4: 5
  - length 5: 1
- Planted syllabic names were Ur III seal-legend names, 1,325 names over 310 syllables, mapped to random home signs.
- Even fully phonetic names of these lengths barely change the two statistics. Planted G2 (any repeated sign) averages 0.11, below the 0.15 bar. Planted G1 (types per token) averages 0.69.
- Names of 2 to 4 syllables drawn from a large syllabary rarely repeat a sign. So short texts cannot show the "small repetitive subset" signature, whether or not they spell names.

## What this means
- Atlas 1.10, the claim that Gulf seals spell foreign names with a small phonetic subset, cannot be tested on the texts we have, whether the set is defined by findspot (n = 12) or by shape (n = 28 residues).
- Its earlier "refuted" verdict should read **untestable** for any realistic syllabic spelling.
- No set of short seal texts can test this. It needs either a known name in a known context (the Meluhha tablet photos) or many more long texts.
