# Novel methods from other fields: results

## Conclusion first

I ran four imported methods on distinct dev texts (1,685 texts with no break mark; line breaks removed). Each was checked against a bigram null, a trigram null and two constructed comparison corpora, then tested on held-out texts (554) and on bridged ICIT texts from sites absent from M77 (122 texts, Dholavira 47).

1. **One new, replicated result: enclosing a fish sign moves it to the end of the text.** Five fish signs have bracketed forms (M59→M60, M65→M66, M67→M68, M70→M71, M72→M73). Plain fish signs end a text 5% of the time. Their bracketed forms end it 60% of the time.
   - Dev: 42 tokens, final share 0.595. Frequency-matched random signs give 0.16 ± 0.12 (p = 0.005).
   - Held-out plus external, tested after the dev finding: 19 tokens, 0.53 against a null of 0.15 (p = 0.03).
   - The bracketed fish still share contexts with their plain bases (cosine p < 0.05 for M60, M66, M68, M73). So the bracket looks like a **modifier that moves a sign into the closer slot**, often after two tall strokes ("M87 M60"). It does not create an unrelated sign. Enclosure of non-fish signs shows no such shift (0.21 against 0.18).
2. **Signs drawn as "jar + strokes inside" do not behave like the jar.** M343, M344 and M345 share no more context with M342 than chance (cosine p = 0.36–0.89), almost never swap with it in identical frames (1 substitution against 1.4 expected), and sit in the opposite place: mid-text or first, where M342 is 70% final. The test is not underpowered: it flags 45 of 45 planted allographs (2.4% false positives). These are separate signs, not "jar + numeral". The two parts also never occur as a sequence ("M99 M342": 0 cases), so the ligature is not an abbreviated bigram. This confirms and sharpens shaunmortal F16.
3. **The fish variants (M65, M67, M70, M72) form one paradigm with plain M59, but they are not free allographs.** They swap in identical frames 10 times against 2.4 expected. Their contexts are more alike than chance (median cosine 0.24 against 0.09). Their similarity is weaker than that of planted allographs (p 0.02–0.10, where planted allographs always give p < 0.05). Inside a 6-sign frame they are mutually exclusive: `M267 M99 [M65|M67|M72] M403 M87 M342`.
4. **Template grammar (profile HMM).** A left-to-right slot model recovers the frame [pre-opener] OPENER CONNECTIVE (fish/number) CORE TITLE CLOSER POST-CLOSER, with readable slot fillers (table below). But no slot model beats the bigram on held-out texts: 5.40 bits per sign for the 12-slot template against 4.87 for the bigram (unigram 6.10).
   - The template captures 57% of the bigram's gain over unigram. That is more than on a corpus generated by the Indus bigram (42%) and about the same as synthetic logo-syllabic seal legends (56%). On a real slot code (licence-plate type) the template beats the bigram outright (124%).
   - **The Indus script sits with language-like name legends, far from an administrative slot code.** Slot identity beyond opener, connective and closer is only moderately reproducible (ARI 0.43 across resamples), in line with shaunmortal M1b.
5. **Minimal pairs (genomics substitution matrices) mostly re-describe the bigram.** The density of edit-distance-1 pairs (0.33 substitutions and 0.18 insertions/deletions per text) and where variation sits (45% first, 32% middle, 24% last, against 20/61/20% of positions) are both reproduced by the bigram null. The substitution classes are real, though, and they predict held-out data. Held-out substitutions match a dev edge 91 times against 36 ± 5 for frequency-matched random pairs (p = 0.002), and fall within a dev class 82 times against 50 ± 6. Indus variation is **front-loaded**, as in the bigram null: opener and initial fish swap. In the synthetic name legends, 70% of variation sits at the end.
6. **"Stemmatics" (column vs tree families) cannot tell the corpora apart.** Indus 0.10 of families are single-column, bigram 0.06 ± 0.04, name legends 0.11, slot code 0.13 ± 0.10. Even on a planted slot code the statistic does not separate, so I make no claim.

Bottom line for the lead: **the frame-and-slot structure is real but is mostly adjacency. The one structural fact the bigram does not give you is sign-internal: a bracket on a fish moves it to the end, and strokes inside a jar make a different sign.** Both are targets for anyone trying to read sign components.

## Data and comparison corpora

- Indus: distinct M77 texts from `dev_heldout()`. Texts with `#` are dropped and `|` is removed. Dev 1,685, held-out 554.
- External test: ICIT texts from sites absent from M77, mapped W→M through the 176-sign bridge, keeping only fully bridged texts. That gives 135 texts (122 not already in dev), from Dholavira 47, Nausharo 13, Banawali 6, Rakhigarhi 5 and others.
- Nulls: an order-1 and order-2 Markov chain fitted on dev, sampling texts of exactly matched length distribution until the distinct-text count matches. Also a unigram token shuffle.
- Synthetic logo-syllabic seal legends: real personal names from `/usr/share/dict/propernames`, syllabified (one token per syllable), in an Old-Akkadian-style template [TITLE]? NAME [son-of NAME]? [OCC]? [servant GOD]?. Titles and occupations are logograms. Mean length 4.5. This stands in for real Sumerian or Akkadian seal legends, which I did not fetch.
- Synthetic slot code: 6 slots with disjoint Zipfian vocabularies, 3 of them optional, and no dependencies between slots.

## Exp 3: morphology inside signs (`exp3_sign_morphology.py`, `exp3b_planted_control.py`)

Sign families are my reading of Mahadevan's drawings:
- enclosure (ticks, bars or parentheses around a sign): 24 pairs
- roof (chevron over a sign): 5 pairs
- strokes inside a jar or cup: 5 pairs
- fish internal marks: 4 pairs
- cup → jar (adding ears): 1 pair

Similarity is the cosine of PPMI left/right-context vectors, against every sign within 0.5–2× the derived sign's frequency.

**Planted control.** Relabel 8% of a frequent sign's tokens as a new sign. The test flags 45 of 45 such planted allographs at p < 0.05. Its false-positive rate on mismatched pairs is 1 in 42.

## Hypotheses tested

| hypothesis | verdict |
|---|---|
| Near-duplicate families exceed what adjacency predicts | refuted (bigram reproduces density; trigram reproduces families) |
| Variation concentrates in particular slots beyond the bigram | refuted (front-loading is reproduced by the bigram) |
| Dev substitution classes predict held-out swaps | **supported** (91 vs 36 ± 5, p = 0.002) |
| An Indus text is a slot code (template beats bigram) | **refuted** (5.40 vs 4.87 bits; a planted slot code beats the bigram) |
| The Indus script is more position-bound than a bigram process | weakly supported (template share 0.57 vs 0.42; single split) |
| Jar + inner strokes (M343–345) = allographs or modifiers of M342 | **refuted** (cosine at null, opposite positions; the test detects 100% of planted allographs) |
| Fish variants M65, M67, M70, M72 are free allographs of M59 | refuted as free variants; supported as one paradigm (10 vs 2.4 substitutions) |
| Enclosure is a consistent modifier moving signs to the end | **supported for fish** (dev p = 0.005, replication p = 0.032); not supported for other enclosed signs |
| "Roof" is a consistent modifier (analogy offset) | inconclusive (p = 0.024 dev, 0.05 test, only 2–3 pairs) |
| Families are trees (copying) rather than columns | inconclusive (statistic fails on planted data) |

**Tests run.**
- Exp1: 12 metrics × 2 nulls, plus 2 held-out tests.
- Exp2: 4 corpora × 12 models (descriptive comparisons).
- Exp3: 5 families × 5 statistics on dev, plus 3 T6 tests on dev and 3 on test.
- Exp3b: one control.
- Exp4: 1 statistic.

The enclosed-fish claim rests on one pre-specified confirmation test (p = 0.032). With Bonferroni over the 3 T6 tests on test, it is p = 0.10, so call it "replicated in direction and size, nominally significant". The effect size (0.53–0.60 against a base of 0.05–0.11) is large.

## Caveats and counterevidence

- The sign-family assignments are my visual reading of Mahadevan's drawings. Mahadevan may have merged or split variants differently from the originals, so check them on CISI photos.
- The enclosed-fish counts are small: 42 dev tokens and 19 test tokens. Part of the effect is the recurring final "M87 M60" (two tall strokes + bracketed fish). If that is one formula repeated across seals, the effective n is smaller. Of the 18 dev texts with M60, 11 end in M60 and 5 end in "M87 M60".
- Counterexamples: M60 is first or middle in about 40% of cases (`M60 M242`, `M70 M60 M99 M86 M197`). Some enclosures move away from the end (M1→M2: 0.74 → 0.00 final). M2 is followed by M12 or M14, a different construction.
- The external test set holds only 122 texts, because of the bridge coverage limit.
- The synthetic name-legend corpus is a stand-in for real Sumerian or Akkadian legends.

**What would falsify the enclosure result:** CISI photos showing that the "brackets" on M60, M66 and the others are seal-edge or field artefacts, or a larger corpus (full ICIT with a complete W↔M bridge) in which enclosed-fish final share falls to the 15% null.

## Next experiments

1. Re-run T6 on full ICIT in Wells numbering. Wells keeps bracketed fish as separate signs, so no bridge is needed and all of Dholavira and Harappa (HARP) can be used.
2. Ask what bracketed fish replace. Do texts ending in a bracketed fish lack M342 more often than length-matched texts? If the bracketed fish fills the closer slot, they should. A first look says yes: they are final and followed only by M176.
3. Use the 78-frame minimal-pair inventory as the target list for any anchor (shaunmortal F35 anchor drill). For example, if any reading of M267-M99 is proposed, the 17 fillers of `M267 M99 [_]` must all make sense in that slot.
4. Run nestedness (NODF) of site repertoires on ICIT, to ask whether small sites draw on a subset of the Mohenjo-daro/Harappa repertoire.
5. Bootstrap CIs for the exp2 template-share comparison, and repeat it with real Akkadian or Sumerian seal legends from CDLI/ORACC.
