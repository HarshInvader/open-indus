Open Indus: three logo options

Each mark is shown at 16, 32, 96 and 200 px, as a lockup with the wordmark, and as a browser-tab icon, in light and dark. All three are one colour, use one stroke weight with round ends, and follow the text colour. The site uses option 1.

1. Fish (chosen, used on the site)

Based on the fish sign (M59), the commonest Indus sign family: one almond body whose two curves cross and run straight on as the tail, with two short fins. The tab icon uses a heavier stroke so it holds at 16 px.

2. Ticked fish (not used)

The fish with four corner ticks (M60). Adding the ticks moves the sign to the end of a text, one of the project's findings. At 16 px the ticks crowd the body.

3. Jar on a seal face

The jar (M342), the commonest sign and the usual last sign of a text, set on a square seal face. Clear at 32 px and up; at 16 px the flags blur.

Each mark is a fresh drawing based on a sign in Mahadevan's 1977 sign list. Using a sign as a logo says nothing about what it means: no Indus sign has a proven sound or meaning.
