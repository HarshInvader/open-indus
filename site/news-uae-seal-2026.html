Reported: a new seal with Indus signs from the UAE

News report (no excavation report yet) · Umm al-Quwain, UAE

In a sentence

A round Gulf-style seal with Indus signs and a humped bull, dated to about 2300 BCE. Not yet verified.

In plain words

A news site reports a small round seal of the Gulf type, with Indus signs and a humped bull, from Umm al-Quwain. The excavation report, the exact find spot and a readable photo of the signs have not been published, so we cannot use it yet.

Technical detail

Known only from a news aggregator. Site (possibly Tell Abraq), excavator and sign sequence not given. Not in our corpus of objects found abroad.

How we read it

Read: not reachable (the news page returns 404 ‘Article not found’; no excavation report was found). Sources opened: ittihaas.com/api/apps/685bf8c05cf93a74890518d0/functions/newsSharePage.

What they did

  • According to a search-result snippet only, the report describes a circular softstone seal with Indus signs and a humped bull from Umm al-Quwain, dated to about 2300 BCE.
  • No primary excavation report was found, so the method of dating and reading is unknown.

What they found

  • Nothing could be read from the source itself.
  • The project’s earlier note found that Majchrzak and Degli Esposti 2024 (PAM 33) describes a different seal, TAD.0105, so the two should not be merged (experiments/uaq_seal).

Strengths and limits

  • A single news item with a dead link is not evidence; the seal’s text, shape and context need a primary publication.
  • Round softstone seals with a bull fit the known Gulf seal type, so the find is plausible but not yet usable.

What the authors say comes next

  • not stated (not stated)

Ideas it gives us

  • Keep a watch entry and, when a primary report appears, add the seal’s sign string to the foreign-seal set and score it under the core-trained grammar model alongside the other Gulf seals. One more Gulf seal with a clear text adds to the small set behind our Gulf penalty result. Open data enough: no. Needs: A published photograph or drawing with a sign reading.
  • Before adding any new Gulf seal, fix in advance the test it will enter: the bits-per-sign penalty under the core model, compared with the existing Gulf set and with SIGIL outpost texts. Pre-registering the test stops a single striking find from being read after the fact. Open data enough: yes. Needs: The existing foreign-seal set and grammar model.

What we take from it

Waiting on data

We looked for an excavation report and found none, and the news page is now offline. It stays out of our data until the find is published.
