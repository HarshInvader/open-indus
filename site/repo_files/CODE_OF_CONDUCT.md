# Code of conduct

{{NAME}} works toward one goal: to {{GOAL_LC}}, in the open. A project like this depends on people telling each other plainly that a result is wrong. That only works if nobody has to fear being attacked for saying it, or for being the one who was wrong.

This code applies in every space the project runs: issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, and any meeting or chat held in the project's name. It also applies when someone represents the project elsewhere.

## What we expect

- **Argue with results, not people.** "This null keeps sign frequencies but not text lengths" is welcome. A remark about someone's competence, motives, nationality or background is not.
- **Show your evidence.** Point to the data, the code or the page. A confident claim with no evidence is not more convincing because it is repeated.
- **Credit others.** Name the person, paper or database a result builds on, including when you disagree with it.
- **Accept being wrong.** A finding that breaks under a fair test is recorded as broken, whoever made it, and that includes the maintainers.
- **Be welcoming to newcomers.** Someone asking a basic question about the script or about statistics is a future contributor.

## Heritage and identity

The Indus script matters to many people and communities in South Asia and beyond, and claims about its language are often tied to claims about identity. Here every proposal about the language is tested the same way, whichever language it names.

- Do not use the project, or its results, to argue that any people, religion, language or nation is superior to another.
- Do not dismiss a proposal because of who made it, or accept one because of who made it.
- Do not mock a reading. If it fails, the bench will show it; say what the result was.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, threats, or insults, in public or in private messages.
- Slurs or demeaning remarks about anyone's ethnicity, religion, caste, nationality, gender, sexual orientation, disability, age or appearance.
- Sharing someone's private information without their permission.
- Repeatedly pushing a claim after it has been tested and answered, to wear people down.
- Presenting someone else's work as your own.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately to: {{CONDUCT_CONTACT}}

Say what happened, where, and when, with links if you have them. Reports are read by the maintainers. A maintainer involved in the report takes no part in handling it. Reports are kept confidential, and we will not retaliate against anyone for reporting in good faith.

## What happens next

Depending on how serious the problem is and whether it has happened before, the maintainers may:

1. ask the person to stop, privately;
2. issue a public warning;
3. ban them from the project for a set time;
4. ban them permanently.

We will tell the person reported what the decision is and why. They may reply once, and the maintainers will consider the reply.
